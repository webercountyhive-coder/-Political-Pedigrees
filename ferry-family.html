The Ferry Family

Who held office, who lobbied, and how the family ties connect in the public record. Box Elder County.
Political Pedigrees › The Ferry Family

Posted: Nov. 13, 2026 · Updated: Nov. 13, 2026 (wording and client details updated after review)

New to this? Read it in plain words About 500 words.
The short version. Three generations of the Ferry family of Corinne (Box Elder County) have served in the Utah Legislature: Miles "Cap" Ferry, his son Ben Ferry, and his grandson Joel Ferry. (Joel is the son of Cap's other son, John; Ben is Joel's uncle.) Cap and his wife Sue were also among Utah's early contract lobbyists, and the lobbying firm Sue started is now run by their grandson David Stewart. Two granddaughters are married to public figures: former senator and MIDA director Dan Hemmert, and state Rep. David Shallenberger. Joel went from the House to running the Department of Natural Resources, served as the state's water agent, and in September 2026 registered as a lobbyist himself. The in-depth look lays out what the public records show, and keeps separate what is still being checked.

Key facts

  • Three generations in the Legislature: Miles "Cap" Ferry (House 1964–67; Senate 1967–84, including as Senate President), his son Ben Ferry, and his grandson Joel Ferry. Joel's father is John Ferry, Ben's brother, so Ben is Joel's uncle.
  • Early contract lobbyists: Cap's wife Sue began contract lobbying about 1980–81 while Cap was Senate President (per Salt Lake Tribune reporting); Cap later joined her. The Hive's copy of the lobbyist register (pulled Oct. 27, 2026) shows both licenses ended in 2017.
  • The family firm: The Salt Lake Tribune reported in 2019 that Legislative Executive Consulting, the firm Sue started, is headed by the Ferrys' grandson David Stewart, with his brother Justin Stewart as a principal. The same story named another lobbyist, Gary Thorup, as the Ferrys' son-in-law, and said Dan Hemmert, then the Senate majority whip, is married to a Ferry granddaughter. A 2016 obituary shows that granddaughter, Natalie, and Rep. David Shallenberger's wife, Lara, are both Jane Ferry Stewart's daughters, sisters of the two lobbyists.
  • Two jobs at once: Joel held his House District 1 seat and was acting DNR director at the same time, from early July to late August 2022.
  • Water agent: Joel was confirmed as the state's water agent Sept. 18, 2024, while still DNR director, and led the state's $30 million acquisition of US Magnesium's land, water rights and mining agreements (not the company itself).
  • Office to lobbying: Joel left DNR Aug. 28, 2026 and registered as a lobbyist Sept. 14, 2026 (Utah Public Solutions; two active client registrations in the Oct. 27, 2026 register snapshot).

Who's who

Miles "Cap" Ferry

Utah House 1964–67; Utah Senate 1967–84, including as Senate President; national NCSL president 1983–84; state Agriculture Commissioner 1985–93. Managed the family ranch. Later joined his wife's lobbying firm. Our lobbyist register shows 16 clients over time; license canceled June 1, 2017. Died March 31, 2017. His obituary lists six children: John, Jane (Stewart), Benjamin, Suzanne (Thorup), Helen and Nora, with 29 grandchildren.

Suzanne "Sue" Ferry

One of Utah's first contract lobbyists, starting about 1980–81, while Cap was Senate President and then Agriculture Commissioner. The Salt Lake Tribune called her "the matriarch of Utah lobbyists." Our register shows 14 clients over time; license canceled Jan. 10, 2017. Died April 28, 2019, at 86.

John Ferry

Cap and Sue's son and Joel's father. Runs J.Y. Ferry & Son, Inc., a ranch and farm near Corinne operating since 1900, with his brother Ben and son Joel. Served on the Utah State University Board of Trustees, including as vice chair before July 2023, with a term listed through 2025; USU gave him its trustees' recognition award in January 2026. Not found in our lobbyist register under his own name.

Ben Ferry

Cap and Sue's son, Joel's uncle. Served in the Utah House 1999–2011 (District 2); did not get the Republican nomination at the 2010 convention, and Lee Perry took the seat. Has chaired the Box Elder County Republican Party and sits on the Utah Republican Party Executive Committee. In 2022 he ran as an independent write-in for House District 1, the seat Joel held, and lost. His own campaign reports (2008–2011) list gifts from his parents, "Suzanne & M.Y. Ferry" ($200 in 2008 and $250 in 2009), and from former House Speaker Greg Curtis ($500, 2009), who later became a principal in Sue's lobbying firm. They also list payments for fuel to J.Y. Ferry & Son, Inc., the family ranch company, and small payments for intern and clerical work to payees with the Ferry name.

David and Justin Stewart

The Ferrys' grandsons, per the Salt Lake Tribune (2019). David heads Legislative Executive Consulting, the firm Sue started (44 active clients, 71 over time in the Hive's Oct. 27, 2026 register copy). Justin, his brother, is a principal (17 active, 41 over time; first registered Dec. 13, 2012). Cap Ferry's 2017 obituary lists a daughter, Jane F. Stewart, and an American Accounting Association memorial says Jane Ferry married BYU accounting professor Dave Nelson Stewart in 1974 and they raised six children. Dave Stewart's 2016 obituary lists their children, including David Levi and Justin Call, so David and Justin are Jane and Dave's sons. (Earlier note, kept: "No record found yet states that David and Justin are Jane and Dave's sons.")

Rep. David Shallenberger

State representative for House District 58. His wife, Lara, is Jane and Dave Stewart's daughter, so she is Cap and Sue Ferry's granddaughter and a sister of lobbyists David and Justin Stewart (Dave Stewart obituary, 2016). His own biography names his wife as Lara.

Dan Hemmert

Former state senator (2016–2021), former GO Utah director, now MIDA's executive director. The Salt Lake Tribune (2019) reported he is married to a Ferry granddaughter; Dave Stewart's 2016 obituary names her as Natalie Suzanne Hemmert, Jane and Dave Stewart's daughter. That makes him brother-in-law to lobbyists David and Justin Stewart. He was registered under Legislative Executive Consulting, the family firm, and his registration for Union Pacific Railroad ended Aug. 3, 2026, six weeks before Joel registered for the same client. See the Hive's Dan Hemmert page.

Gary Thorup

The Ferrys' son-in-law, per the Salt Lake Tribune (2019). A tax attorney and registered lobbyist with Dentons Durham Jones Pinegar (19 active clients, 29 over time; first registered Jan. 25, 2013, in the Hive's Oct. 27, 2026 register copy). His wife is Suzanne Ferry: the 1993 Deseret News obituary for his father, Robert Smith Thorup, lists "Gary Richard (Suzanne Ferry)." (Earlier note, kept: "Which Ferry daughter he married isn't stated in the records found.") That obituary doesn't name her parents; the Tribune's 2019 story is what calls Gary the Ferrys' son-in-law.

Johnny Ferry

Ran for Utah Senate District 19 (Weber, Morgan and part of Davis counties) in 2020 and lost the Republican primary to John Johnson, 43.6% to 56.4% (Ballotpedia). His conflict-of-interest form lists Pleasant View as home, and work as vice president of business development at Honeyville, Inc., an Ogden food manufacturer, plus a seat on the Utah Manufacturers Association board. His campaign report lists $5,000 from Teresa Ferry of Corinne with the note "Thanks Mom." John Ferry's wife is Teresa (Cap Ferry obituary), so Johnny appears to be Joel's brother. Being checked until a record states it. Donors to his race included Joel, two of Joel's aunts and Dan Hemmert (see his donors). He was sworn in to the Pleasant View City Council on Jan. 13, 2026, the same night Steve Gibson was sworn in as mayor (Pleasant View council minutes; see the Gibson page).

Joel Ferry

Former Zions Bank banker; Box Elder County GOP chairman. Elected to House District 1 in 2018, succeeding Scott Sandall. Named Department of Natural Resources director in June 2022; confirmed Sept. 21, 2022. Appointed state water agent, confirmed Sept. 18, 2024. Vice chair of the Colorado River Authority of Utah. Left DNR Aug. 28, 2026; registered as a lobbyist Sept. 14, 2026 (Utah Public Solutions; two active client registrations in the Oct. 27, 2026 register snapshot: Union Pacific Railroad and Utah Medical Association). Union Pacific worked with DNR on the 2023 causeway berm while he ran the department.

The seat that stayed in the family's corner

House District 1 (Box Elder County) went from Scott Sandall to Joel Ferry in 2018. When Ferry moved to DNR in 2022, he resigned after the ballot filing deadline, stayed on the November ballot, won with 82%, and resigned again, so the seat was filled by appointment both times (Rep. Thomas Peterson). Sandall moved to the Senate and is now vice chair of Executive Appropriations.

The lobbying firm

From 2013 to 2017, Cap and Sue Ferry were registered under Legislative Executive Consulting, the firm of David Stewart (44 active clients, 71 over time as of Oct. 27, 2026). Also registered under that firm: Justin Stewart, former House Speaker Greg Curtis, and later Dan Hemmert (former GO Utah director, now MIDA's executive director).

The firm is a business tie on the record, and now a family tie too: the Salt Lake Tribune reported in 2019 that David Stewart, who heads the firm, is the Ferrys' grandson, and that his brother Justin is a principal. (Earlier note, kept: "A family tie between the Ferrys and the Stewarts has been described to the Hive but is not yet in any record found.") Words to know

  • Contract lobbyist: someone paid by outside clients (companies, cities, groups) to talk with lawmakers and officials for them. Utah lobbyists must register each client with the state.
  • Registration: the state record that a lobbyist works for a client, with start and end dates.
  • Acting director: someone running an agency before the Senate has confirmed them for the job.
  • Water agent: a state job created by S.B. 211 (2024) to plan for Utah's future water supply. The governor appoints and the Senate confirms.
  • Write-in candidate: a candidate whose name isn't printed on the ballot; voters write it in.
  • Appointed seat: when a legislator leaves mid-term, party delegates pick a replacement and the governor appoints them.

Common questions

Is it against the rules for relatives to lobby?

No Utah rule found bars relatives of officials from lobbying. Utah's lobbyist law requires registration and reports of gifts and spending. This page shows the family ties so readers can weigh them.

Did Joel Ferry do anything for his family's clients?

No record found shows that. The table of registrations shows who the family's lobbyists worked for, not what any official did. That's why it's listed as a question to ask, not a finding.
