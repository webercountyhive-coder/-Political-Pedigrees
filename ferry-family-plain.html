The Ferry Family, in plain words

A short, simple version. The full page has every date, record and link.

Who they are

The Ferrys are a ranching family from Corinne, in Box Elder County. Three generations have served in the Utah Legislature: Miles "Cap" Ferry, his son Ben, and his grandson Joel.

The family business is lobbying, too

A lobbyist is someone paid by companies, cities or groups to talk with lawmakers for them. Cap's wife, Sue, was one of Utah's first paid lobbyists, starting around 1980. She started a lobbying firm, and Cap joined it after he left office.

Today that firm is run by two of Cap and Sue's grandsons, David and Justin Stewart. A son-in-law, Gary Thorup, is also a lobbyist. A newspaper reported in 2019 that former state senator Dan Hemmert is married to one of their granddaughters.

Joel Ferry's path

Joel was elected to the Utah House in 2018. In 2022 the governor picked him to run the Department of Natural Resources (DNR), which manages the state's land, water and wildlife. For about two months he was in the House and running DNR at the same time. In 2024 he also became the state's first "water agent," a job that plans for Utah's future water.

Joel left DNR on Aug. 28, 2026. Seventeen days later, he registered as a lobbyist himself.

What we don't know yet

While Joel ran DNR, his cousin David Stewart was registered to lobby for several water districts and other clients tied to land, water and energy. A registration only shows who hired a lobbyist. It doesn't show what was discussed or that Joel did anything for them. We haven't found any record of that.

Two of Cap and Sue's granddaughters are married to public figures: former state senator Dan Hemmert, who now runs the Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA), and state Rep. David Shallenberger. Both women are sisters of the two lobbyists (Dave Stewart's 2016 obituary).

Two cities that hired family lobbyists got state water money soon after. Highland's $1 million came from GO Utah in late 2022, while Dan Hemmert still ran that office. Tremonton hired Hemmert's firm in February 2023, and that June a water board under Joel's department gave the city $2 million. The records don't show who chose those grants or that Joel or Hemmert had a role.

In 2024, a division of Joel's department handed out about $22 million in water grants. Several cities and water districts that won had hired family lobbyists. The records don't show who scored the applications or that Joel had any role.

There may be one more family member in politics. Johnny Ferry of Pleasant View ran for the state Senate in 2020 and lost. His campaign report lists $5,000 from Teresa Ferry with the note "Thanks Mom," and Joel's mother is named Teresa, so Johnny appears to be Joel's brother. We're still checking that. Joel, two of his aunts and Dan Hemmert all gave to Johnny's campaign.

A newspaper also reported in 2025 that Joel's name was on two state farm water grants, about $205,000, for a farm owned by his cousin Ben Ferry. Joel said his name made no difference and that he stayed out of the votes on them.

Questions you can ask

  • Did any of the family's lobbying clients have business before DNR while Joel ran it? Ask DNR or check its meeting minutes.
  • Who are Joel's lobbying clients now, and what do they want from the state? Check lobbyist.utah.gov.

Words to know

  • Lobbyist: a person paid to speak to lawmakers for someone else.
  • Water agent: a state job that plans for Utah's future water supply.
  • Being checked: something we've been told but haven't found in a public record yet.
  • Grant: money the state gives a city or district for a project, which doesn't have to be paid back.
